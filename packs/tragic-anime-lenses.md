# Tragic Anime Story Engine — Secondary Reference Lenses

Companion to `packs/tragic-anime-engine.md`. Use these as **craft lenses**, not imitation presets — same non-imitation rule as the primary pillars: no proprietary names, characters, plot sequences, or distinctive dialogue.

---

## Puella Magi Madoka Magica — Hope/Despair Trap

Transferable mechanisms: innocent desire becomes structurally dangerous; wishes have hidden system-level cost; hope and despair are mechanically connected; sacrifice changes metaphysical rules; repeated attempts create tragic knowledge.

Use for: bargains, magical contracts, hidden cost systems, loops, "saving someone may worsen the system" stories.

## Devilman Crybaby — Empathy vs. Social Collapse

Transferable mechanisms: supernatural threat amplifies human paranoia; society becomes part of the antagonist; fear destroys trust; emotional tenderness exists beside apocalypse; tragedy comes from dehumanization, not merely monsters.

Use for: mass panic, witch hunts, identity persecution, apocalyptic tragedy.

## Cyberpunk: Edgerunners — Systemic Doom and Self-Consumption

Transferable mechanisms: protagonist wants a life the environment is designed to deny; power/capability expansion accelerates self-destruction; dreams are inherited between characters; love creates an alternative future that may remain unreachable; the system survives individual rebellion.

Use for: class tragedy, doomed ambition, body-cost escalation, "becoming exceptional destroys you" arcs.

## 86 EIGHTY-SIX — Institutional Dehumanization

Transferable mechanisms: a protected population benefits from hidden sacrifice; language/naming systems reduce people to categories; distance makes cruelty easier; human connection crosses structural separation; war bureaucracy becomes a moral actor.

Use for: military dystopia, caste systems, expendable soldiers, remote command guilt.

## To Your Eternity — Repeated Attachment and Loss

Transferable mechanisms: immortality does not remove grief; each relationship adds to identity; loss becomes memory and capability; recurring death can work when each bond is different; the protagonist changes through accumulated people.

Use for: immortal protagonists, long timelines, memory-based powers, stories about carrying the dead forward.

## Fate/Zero — Ideals in a Zero-Sum System

Transferable mechanisms: multiple adult protagonists hold incompatible ideals; goals are clear before collision; victories expose ideological weakness; noble intentions can produce horrific methods; tragic irony grows from character philosophy.

Use for: tournaments, faction wars, ideological ensembles, doomed strategists.

## Made in Abyss — Irreversible Descent

Transferable mechanisms: wonder and horror coexist; deeper exploration increases irreversible cost; the world has rules that punish retreat; curiosity is both beautiful and dangerous; the environment itself generates narrative pressure.

Use for: layered worlds, expeditions, cursed zones, "deeper means less return" stories.

## Violet Evergarden — Aftermath and Emotional Reconstruction

Transferable mechanisms: tragedy continues after the war ends; episodic encounters refract the protagonist's unresolved emotion; communication itself becomes plot; grief can be quiet rather than violent; healing does not erase the past.

Use for: postwar stories, emotional aftermath, letters/messages, recovery arcs, tragedies where nobody needs to die on-page.

## Chainsaw Man — Small Desire Inside Brutal Systems

Transferable mechanisms: the protagonist's desires can be mundane rather than grand; deprivation makes ordinary experiences emotionally huge; institutions exploit need; absurd humor can sit beside horror; identity and intimacy can be manipulated through power imbalance.

Use for: exploited young protagonists, grotesque action mixed with human comedy, stories where "normal life" is the highest aspiration.

---

## Design conclusion

The strongest shared lesson across all nine secondary lenses, and the two primary pillars, is not "kill characters." It is: **create something worth preserving, put it under coherent pressure, make characters choose, enforce the cost, and let the consequences remain in the story.**
