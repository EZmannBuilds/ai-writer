### `va-moral-injury`

- **Source:** U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, National Center for PTSD, "Moral Injury" (professional page) and "Moral Injury and PTSD." https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/cooccurring/moral_injury.asp ; https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand/related/moral_injury_ptsd.asp
- **Type:** clinical-public-information
- **Status:** recorded in the pack's v1.0 research notes (2026-09-15); not re-verified in v0.6
- **Claim:** Moral injury is distress that can follow acting, failing to act, witnessing, or experiencing betrayal around events that violate deeply held values; guilt, shame, anger, betrayal, and difficulty with self-forgiveness may be involved.
- **Derives:** The military module separates the event from the character's moral interpretation of it, and splits actual, perceived, and institutional responsibility.
- **Limitations:** Clinical public information about real people. Used to make fiction plausible, never to diagnose a character.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `bandura-1999`

- **Source:** Bandura, "Moral disengagement in the perpetration of inhumanities," *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 1999 (PubMed 15661671); see also Bandura et al., 1996, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.71.2.364. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15661671/
- **Type:** theoretical
- **Status:** recorded in the pack's v1.0 research notes (2026-09-15); not re-verified in v0.6
- **Claim:** People disengage moral self-sanctions through mechanisms including moral justification, displacement and diffusion of responsibility, minimizing consequences, blaming victims, and dehumanization.
- **Derives:** The villain belief engine: villainy develops through concrete rationalization channels rather than a generic "became evil" beat.
- **Limitations:** Social-psychological theory about real perpetrators; applied to fictional characters as a lens.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `narrative-identity-reviews`

- **Source:** Dunlop et al., 2020 (PubMed 33023390), and a narrative-identity review (PMC4395856), as listed in the pack's research notes. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33023390/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4395856/
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** recorded in the pack's v1.0 research notes (2026-09-15); not re-verified in v0.6. Titles and authors beyond these identifiers were not recorded; check before quoting.
- **Claim:** Narrative-identity research analyzes how people narrate turning points through themes such as agency, communion, redemption, and contamination.
- **Derives:** The Meaning Crisis stage: a character turns an event into an explanation, and that story predicts behavior. No mechanical link to morality is implied.
- **Limitations:** Real-person life-story research; bibliographic details incomplete here.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `apa-growth-after-trauma`

- **Source:** American Psychological Association, *Monitor on Psychology*, "Growth after trauma," November 2016. https://www.apa.org/monitor/2016/11/growth-trauma
- **Type:** clinical-public-information
- **Status:** recorded in the pack's v1.0 research notes (2026-09-15); not re-verified in v0.6
- **Claim:** Adversity can be followed by changes in meaning, priorities, relationships, and self-understanding; trauma does not point only toward corruption.
- **Derives:** Same-event divergence; hero origins through growth; no automatic line from trauma to villainy.
- **Limitations:** Magazine coverage of research, not a primary study.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual
