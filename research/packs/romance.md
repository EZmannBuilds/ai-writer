# Research: Romance / Attachment

Pack: [packs/romance.md](../../packs/romance.md). Research pass 2026-09-16.

**Scope.** Relationship science studies real people — mostly North American or Western students and married couples, often heterosexual. The pack uses it as a lens for believable characters, never as a diagnosis, and doesn't assign findings by gender.

**Gaps.** No organizational consent guideline for fiction and no verified slow-burn craft book were checked. Regis's eight elements rest on search text plus partial confirmation; check the book before quoting them as a list.

## Relationship science

### `hazan-shaver-1987`

- **Source:** Hazan & Shaver, "Romantic love conceptualized as an attachment process," *JPSP* 52(3), 511–524, 1987. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.52.3.511
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (metadata and abstract)
- **Claim:** Romantic love can be framed as an attachment bond; secure, avoidant, and anxious/ambivalent patterns appear in adults and shape love experiences.
- **Derives:** Characters have a characteristic stance toward closeness — as a tendency, not a type.
- **Limitations:** Self-report; categorical measure later replaced by dimensions; WEIRD sample.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `mikulincer-shaver-2003`

- **Source:** Mikulincer & Shaver, "The Attachment Behavioral System in Adulthood," *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 35, 53–152, 2003. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(03)01002-5
- **Type:** theoretical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (authors' draft read)
- **Claim:** Threat activates proximity seeking; when a partner is unavailable, people tend toward hyperactivation (protest, pursuit) or deactivation (withdrawal, compulsive self-reliance); threat suppresses exploration, sex, caregiving, and empathy until security returns.
- **Derives:** The threat-response record; frightened partners give less care until safe; safe-harbor scenes.
- **Limitations:** A review built on lab and self-report studies; describes tendencies.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `sternberg-1986`

- **Source:** Sternberg, "A triangular theory of love," *Psychological Review* 93(2), 119–135, 1986. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.93.2.119
- **Type:** theoretical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (metadata; component names via a later study)
- **Claim:** Love comprises intimacy, passion, and decision/commitment.
- **Derives:** Separate dimensions that move independently.
- **Limitations:** Theory; the named love types were not checked.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `sorokowski-2021`

- **Source:** Sorokowski et al., "Universality of the Triangular Theory of Love," *Journal of Sex Research*, 2021. https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2020.1787318
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (Europe PMC record)
- **Claim:** The three-factor structure held across more than 7,300 participants in 25 countries, and components varied with relationship length.
- **Derives:** Cross-cultural support for the dimensional model.
- **Limitations:** Self-report.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `rusbult-1983`

- **Source:** Rusbult, "A longitudinal test of the investment model," *JPSP* 45(1), 101–117, 1983. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.45.1.101
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (full text)
- **Claim:** Commitment rises with satisfaction, with poorer alternatives, and with investment; commitment predicted staying or leaving; some people stayed heavily invested despite low satisfaction.
- **Derives:** Commitment has causes; entrapment; rivals raise alternatives; shared history raises the cost of leaving.
- **Limitations:** US college dating couples; heterosexual; self-report.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `aron-1997`

- **Source:** Aron, Melinat, Aron, Vallone & Bator, "The Experimental Generation of Interpersonal Closeness," *PSPB* 23(4), 363–377, 1997. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (abstract)
- **Claim:** Gradually escalating reciprocal self-disclosure produced more closeness than small talk.
- **Derives:** The disclosure ladder; slow burn paced by vulnerability.
- **Limitations:** Strangers in a lab; closeness, not love. The popular "makes people fall in love" claim overstates it.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `laurenceau-1998`

- **Source:** Laurenceau, Barrett & Pietromonaco, "Intimacy as an interpersonal process," *JPSP* 74(5), 1238–1251, 1998. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.74.5.1238
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (abstract)
- **Claim:** Intimacy came from both partners' disclosure, emotional more than factual, and was partly explained by perceived partner responsiveness (a test of Reis & Shaver's model).
- **Derives:** A disclosure only builds intimacy when the response lands; a dismissed confession lowers trust.
- **Limitations:** Diary self-report; university samples.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `murray-1996`

- **Source:** Murray, Holmes & Griffin, "The self-fulfilling nature of positive illusions in romantic relationships," *JPSP* 71(6), 1155–1180, 1996. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.71.6.1155
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (abstract)
- **Claim:** Partners who idealized each other had more stable, satisfying relationships, and people came to see themselves as their partner did.
- **Derives:** Each partner holds their own picture of the relationship; the pictures shape outcomes; the perception gap is an engine.
- **Limitations:** Canadian dating couples; self-report; idealization helps within limits.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `gottman-1998`

- **Source:** Gottman, Coan, Carrère & Swanson, "Predicting Marital Happiness and Stability from Newlywed Interactions," *Journal of Marriage and the Family* 60(1), 5–22, 1998. https://doi.org/10.2307/353438
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (metadata); predictor details from search summaries
- **Claim:** In 130 newlywed couples, conflict-interaction features predicted later stability and satisfaction; reported predictors include harsh start-up, failed de-escalation, and rejecting a partner's influence.
- **Derives:** Conflict beats: start, de-escalation, accepting influence.
- **Limitations:** Heterosexual US newlyweds; gendered findings not transferred; accuracy figures criticized.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `gottman-levenson-1992`

- **Source:** Gottman & Levenson, "Marital processes predictive of later dissolution," *JPSP* 63(2), 221–233, 1992. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.63.2.221
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (abstract)
- **Claim:** Couples with poorly regulated conflict showed more defensiveness and withdrawal, worse health, and more thoughts of separation over four years.
- **Derives:** Defensiveness and withdrawal as conflict signals.
- **Limitations:** 73 couples.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `baumeister-1993`

- **Source:** Baumeister, Wotman & Stillwell, "Unrequited love: On heartbreak, anger, guilt, scriptlessness, and humiliation," *JPSP* 64(3), 377–394, 1993. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.64.3.377
- **Type:** empirical
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (metadata); content via summaries and an encyclopedia entry
- **Claim:** Accounts from both sides: would-be lovers recalled highs and lows and often believed the feeling was mutual; rejecters reported guilt, annoyance, and not knowing how to refuse.
- **Derives:** Write unrequited love from both sides; clarity of rejection; the rejecter isn't a villain by default.
- **Limitations:** Retrospective student narratives; content secondhand.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

## Genre scholarship and definitions

### `regis-2003`

- **Source:** Pamela Regis, *A Natural History of the Romance Novel*, University of Pennsylvania Press, 2003; and Regis, "Ten Years After," *Journal of Popular Romance Studies*, 2013. https://www.jprstudies.org/2013/06/ten-years-after-a-natural-history-of-the-romance-novel-thinking-back-looking-forward-by-pamela-regis/
- **Type:** genre-scholarship
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (JPRS article; book via Google Books record); the eight-element list is partly confirmed
- **Claim:** Romance novels contain recurring elements — including society defined, meeting, barrier, attraction, declaration, point of ritual death, recognition, betrothal — which can appear in any order, repeat, and share scenes.
- **Derives:** Story beats as a reorderable set; dark moment at the barrier's height paired with recognition.
- **Limitations:** Built mainly on heterosexual Anglo-American novels; betrothal needs adapting.
- **Fidelity:** convention

### `rwa-genre-definition`

- **Source:** Romance Writers of America, "The Romance Genre." https://www.rwa.org/the-romance-genre
- **Type:** craft-practice
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16
- **Claim:** A romance has a central love story and an emotionally satisfying and optimistic ending.
- **Derives:** The romance-genre ending contract. (HEA/HFN are community terms, not used on the page.)
- **Limitations:** An industry definition; the organization's standing has been contested.
- **Fidelity:** convention

### `wood-allan-2016`

- **Source:** Wood & Allan, "Queering Popular Romance" (editors' introduction), *Journal of Popular Romance Studies*, 2016. https://www.jprstudies.org/2016/07/special-issue-queering-popular-romance-editors-introductionby-andrea-wood-and-jonathan-a-allan/
- **Type:** genre-scholarship
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16
- **Claim:** LGBTQ romance is wanted by readers but peripheral in scholarship; contributors critique homonormative defaults in mainstream gay romance.
- **Derives:** Check queer storylines for unexamined heterosexual defaults.
- **Limitations:** An editorial introduction.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `kies-2024`

- **Source:** Bridget Kies, "Saying 'I Don't': Queer Romance in the Post–Marriage Equality World," *Journal of Popular Romance Studies*, 2024. https://www.jprstudies.org/2024/03/saying-i-dont-queer-romance-in-the-post-marriage-equality-world/
- **Type:** genre-scholarship
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16
- **Claim:** Recent LGBTQ romances replace the betrothal with anti-proposals or non-marital commitment and still satisfy.
- **Derives:** Commitment in the characters' own form; don't hard-code marriage.
- **Limitations:** A few recent US/UK case studies.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `popova-2018`

- **Source:** Milena Popova, "Rewriting the Romance: Emotion Work and Consent in Arranged Marriage Fanfiction," *Journal of Popular Romance Studies*, 2018. https://www.jprstudies.org/2018/07/rewriting-the-romance-emotion-work-and-consent-in-arranged-marriage-fanfictionby-milena-popova/
- **Type:** genre-scholarship
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16
- **Claim:** These stories name structural imbalances that undermine consent and make consent meaningful by having the more powerful partner do the emotional work of reading signals.
- **Derives:** Power-aware consent; the more powerful character checks in.
- **Limitations:** Close readings of two fanfiction texts.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `kamble-2014`

- **Source:** Jayashree Kamblé, *Making Meaning in Popular Romance Fiction: An Epistemology*, Palgrave Macmillan, 2014 (via Heiss's review, JPRS 2016). https://www.jprstudies.org/2016/07/review-making-meaning-in-popular-romance-fiction-an-epistemology-by-jayashree-kamble/
- **Type:** genre-scholarship
- **Status:** verified 2026-09-16 (review)
- **Claim:** Reads the romance hero as carrying a society's shifting values about capitalism, war, heterosexuality, and whiteness.
- **Derives:** A love interest expresses the world's values; building one is also worldbuilding.
- **Limitations:** Known through a review that notes gaps in its selection and definitions.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual
