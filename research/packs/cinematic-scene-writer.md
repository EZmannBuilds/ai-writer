## Entries

### `august-scene-process`

- **Source:** John August, “How to write a scene,” 2007-01-09. https://johnaugust.com/2007/write-scene
- **Type:** professional-craft
- **Status:** Text/PDF consulted 2026-09-26; pack-design entries authored locally.
- **Claim:** August asks what a scene needs to accomplish, what its removal would cost, who belongs in it, where it occurs and how much screen time it deserves. He considers alternate beginnings and imagines the action before writing a full scene.
- **Derives:** Scene necessity, deliberate length and the distinction between an outline and complete scene prose.
- **Limitations:** One professional workflow; his strong removal test is a diagnostic, not a ban on texture or contemplative duration.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `academy-screen-format`

- **Source:** Academy Nicholl, Greg Beal / April Rider, “For a Few Days More” screenplay format guide, revised 2014. https://www.oscars.org/sites/oscars/files/scriptsample.pdf
- **Type:** institutional-format-guide
- **Status:** Text/PDF consulted 2026-09-26; pack-design entries authored locally.
- **Claim:** The sample demonstrates interior/exterior headings, action and dialogue layout, short action paragraphs and page continuity. It distinguishes submission scripts from shooting scripts and makes readability the underlying goal.
- **Derives:** Legible screen action and an explicit choice between conventional screenplay and annotated directing formats.
- **Limitations:** US screenplay conventions; not a universal music-video treatment format or proof that any page lasts one minute.
- **Fidelity:** convention

### `caston-music-video-forms`

- **Source:** Emily Caston, “A short history of the British music video,” BFI, 2020-12-03. https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/short-history-british-music-video
- **Type:** historical-research
- **Status:** Text/PDF consulted 2026-09-26; pack-design entries authored locally.
- **Claim:** Caston describes performance, concept and narrative forms and their overlapping histories. The account also connects music-video practice with image-led and silent-film storytelling.
- **Derives:** Choose the form appropriate to the song; narrative is available without being compulsory.
- **Limitations:** A British historical account, not a scene-writing experiment or a taxonomy that forbids hybrids.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `schroder-pille`

- **Source:** Johannes Schröder interview, “Pille aus Luft,” Directors Notes, 2016-08-24. https://directorsnotes.com/2016/08/24/johannes-schroder-pille-aus-luft/
- **Type:** director-interview
- **Status:** Text/PDF consulted 2026-09-26; pack-design entries authored locally.
- **Claim:** Schröder describes a detailed treatment, toy-figure previsualization and rehearsals against the track. A continuous camera move and different performance speeds allowed scenes to unfold within one shot.
- **Derives:** Check action duration and blocking against music; do not equate animation or intensity with frequent cuts.
- **Limitations:** A specific one-shot production; its frame rates and camera design are not general prescriptions.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `usberghi-sanctuary`

- **Source:** Stefano Usberghi interview, “Sanctuary,” Directors Notes, 2024-08-08. https://directorsnotes.com/2024/08/08/stefano-usberghi-sanctuary/
- **Type:** director-interview
- **Status:** Text/PDF consulted 2026-09-26; pack-design entries authored locally.
- **Claim:** Usberghi describes planning shots from a blueprint through blocking, beginning with static wide views and changing to more dynamic movement and cutting when the track takes off.
- **Derives:** Make changes in film language serve a planned dramatic and musical escalation.
- **Limitations:** One techno narrative video; rotations, spins and effects are examples, not required aesthetics.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `adin-mcnally-rocket-man`

- **Source:** Majid Adin and Stephen McNally interview, “Rocket Man,” Directors Notes, 2017-06-01. https://directorsnotes.com/2017/06/01/majid-adin-stephen-mcnally-rocket-man/
- **Type:** director-interview
- **Status:** Text/PDF consulted 2026-09-26; pack-design entries authored locally.
- **Claim:** Adin relates the song to personal experience. McNally describes developing scene thumbnails into a rough music-timed animatic, refining structure, and briefing animators on performance before detailed animation.
- **Derives:** Keep emotional interpretation specific; distinguish written scenes, timed staging and finished acting.
- **Limitations:** Their particular animated film and collaborative pipeline; no reference story or imagery is transferred.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual

### `frost-prelude`

- **Source:** Harv Frost interview, “Prelude to Ecstasy,” Directors Notes, 2024-10-11. https://directorsnotes.com/2024/10/11/harv-frost-prelude-to-ecstasy/
- **Type:** director-interview
- **Status:** Text/PDF consulted 2026-09-26; pack-design entries authored locally.
- **Claim:** Frost describes connecting artist-supplied concepts into an overall film while building setups and payoffs within the individual song pieces.
- **Derives:** Let local scene payoffs and a longer emotional throughline coexist; preserve the artist’s stated concerns.
- **Limitations:** An album film built with its band; not a mandatory multi-song structure for a single track.
- **Fidelity:** conceptual
