## Research: show vs tell / scene craft

### Jane Friedman — What Do We Really Mean When We Say "Show, Don't Tell"?
https://janefriedman.com/what-do-we-really-mean-when-we-say-show-dont-tell/

Supports:
- telling remains useful;
- showing matters in high-value scene moments;
- reader immersion depends on action/emotion clarity.

### Reedsy / Mary Kole — Beyond "Show, Don't Tell"
https://reedsy.com/learning/events/beyond-show-dont-tell

Supports:
- show/tell balance;
- telling can control pacing, interiority, voice;
- rule should not be treated absolutely.

### Reedsy Live — Anatomy of a Great Scene
https://reedsy.com/live/anatomy-of-a-great-scene/

Supports:
- scene goal;
- conflict/resistance;
- stakes;
- scene change;
- transitions;
- show/tell balance.

### Jane Friedman — Writing Scenes: Crafting the Setup and the Payoff
https://janefriedman.com/writing-scenes-setup-payoff/

Supports:
- scene and exposition can work together;

## Research: AI narrative defaults

### Russell et al. (2026) — StoryScope: Investigating idiosyncrasies in AI fiction
https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03136

Supports:
- AI stories over-explain themes more often;
- favor tidier single-track plots;
- human stories show more moral ambiguity and temporal complexity;
- discourse-level choices distinguish AI/human corpora strongly.

Important:
These are aggregate corpus findings, not a recipe for "human writing."
v0.4 uses them as anti-default diagnostics only.
